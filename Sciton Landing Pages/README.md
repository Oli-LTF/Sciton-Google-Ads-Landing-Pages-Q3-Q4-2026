# Sciton UK — Google Ads landing pages

Landing pages for the Sciton UK B2B Google Ads programme (Q3–Q4 2026). Built to
read as an extension of `sciton.uk`: the theme stylesheet, Aeonik webfonts, icon
sprite and component markup are taken from the live site.

`index.html` at the root is the front door: a listing page with a card per
campaign, built for presenting the work rather than for traffic. It is what a
Vercel deployment serves at `/`.

**Built so far:**

| Campaign | Page | Ad-group angle |
|---|---|---|
| `C1 — Sciton Brand` | `c1-sciton-brand/index.html` | JOULE modularity — one platform, many applications |
| `C2 — LHR Equipment Purchase` | `c2-lhr-equipment/index.html` | OMNI — throughput, all skin types, no consumables |
| `C3 — Fractional & Resurfacing` | `c3-fractional-resurfacing/index.html` | HALO + ProFractional — hybrid resurfacing, Er:YAG depth, erbium vs CO2 |
| `C4 — Competitor: Cynosure` | `c4-competitor-cynosure/index.html` | Breadth — treatments per chassis, upgrade path, consumables |
| `C5 — Competitor: Fotona` | `c5-competitor-fotona/index.html` | Resurfacing depth — wavelengths, microns, coagulation control |
| `C6 — Competitor: Lumenis / Cutera / Candela` | `c6-competitor-platforms/index.html` | Single-system limits — light and laser on one platform |

assets/js/lp.js               reveals, accordion, scrollspy, form (~230 lines)
assets/img/                   product photography pulled from sciton.uk
                              (`omni-*` from the OMNI product page; `halo-*`,
                              `pf-*` and `ba-halo-*` / `ba-pf-*` from the HALO
                              and ProFractional product and gallery pages)
assets/img/preview-c*.jpg     card previews on the listing page — real
                              screenshots, regenerated by .claude/shots.py
assets/sprite.svg             34-icon subset of the site's sprite (reference copy)
.claude/serve.py              local static server
.claude/shoot.py              headless-Chrome section screenshots (dev aid)
.claude/shots.py              regenerates the six listing-page previews

The listing page has no sprite, no form and no sticky CTA — it uses the theme,
`lp.css` and the reveal half of `lp.js` and nothing else. If a page's hero
changes, rerun `.claude/shots.py` (with the server running) so its card
preview matches.

`sciton-theme.css` is the site's production Tailwind build. Its `@font-face`
rules use relative paths (`../fonts/Aeonik-*.woff2`), which is why the CSS and
fonts sit in sibling folders — do not move one without the other.

`lp.css` adds only what the theme lacks and is annotated section by section.
It introduces no new colours, radii or shadows.

## Deploying

The root `index.html` means a plain static deployment serves the listing page
at `/`, with no configuration and no build step. The card links are explicit
`.../index.html` paths, so they resolve with or without clean URLs.

`.vercelignore` keeps three things out of the deployment, and they matter:

* **the two campaign PDFs** — client planning material, which would otherwise
  sit on a public URL;
* **`Aeonik/`** — the licensed OTF/TTF source files. The pages use the subset
  `assets/fonts/Aeonik-*.woff2`, which are *not* ignored. Do not add them to
  that file or every page loses its typeface;
* **`.claude/`, `.agents/`** — local tooling.

Every page carries `noindex, nofollow`, so a preview URL cannot be indexed
while the work is being reviewed.

## Before these pages go live

Items 1, 2 and 6 apply to **all six** pages. Items 3, 4 and 5 are
page-specific. Section 7 applies only to the three competitor pages and is the
most important one on this list.

### 1. Testimonials — placeholder content

All six pages carry three quotes in a **From UK clinic owners** section. They are
written to the right shape but are **not real**. They are marked in two ways:

* `data-placeholder="true"` on each `<li class="lp-quote">`
* a small `PLACEHOLDER` label rendered in the card corner

Replace the quote, name and role, then delete the `data-placeholder` attribute
(the corner label disappears with it). If real quotes aren't available at
launch, delete the whole `#reviews` section rather than shipping placeholders.

The campaign strategy specifically calls for these:

> "A good example of a landing page will be current clinic owners with
> testimonials on how the Sciton laser grew their clinic client base"

### 2. Form endpoint — not connected

Each page's `#demo-form` validates client-side and then stops. Both carry
`action="#"` and `data-endpoint="TODO"`.

To wire it up, edit `initForm()` in `assets/js/lp.js` — the `TODO` comment
marks the spot. Client-side validation, focus management and the analytics
event already work and should be kept.

On a valid submit the page pushes:

```js
window.dataLayer.push({
  event: 'generate_lead',
  form_id: 'demo-form',
  campaign: '<the form's data-campaign>',   // C1-sciton-brand | C2-lhr-equipment | C3-fractional-resurfacing
  product_interest: '<selected option>',
  budget: '<selected budget band>',
  marketing_opt_in: <true|false>
});
```

`campaign` is read from `data-campaign` on the `<form>`, so one shared
`lp.js` serves every page and a new page only has to set the attribute. The six
values in use are `C1-sciton-brand`, `C2-lhr-equipment`,
`C3-fractional-resurfacing`, `C4-competitor-cynosure`, `C5-competitor-fotona`
and `C6-competitor-platforms`.

**Two separate consents.** `consent` is required and covers replying to the
enquiry. `marketing` is a distinct, optional, unticked opt-in for email
marketing — it must stay that way to satisfy UK GDPR / PECR, so do not
pre-tick it, bundle it into the required consent, or make it a condition of
submitting. Whatever you wire the form to must record the two separately.

`budget` is a band selector — £30k–£200k+ on C1, C3 and C4–C6, £20k–£100k+ on
C2, matching each page's audience. The competitor pages use C1's bands because
they are platform enquiries rather than single-box ones. C3 uses C1's bands because HALO and
ProFractional are handpieces on the JOULE platform, so the enquiry is often a
platform purchase rather than a single box. It is deliberately **optional** — a
required budget question is the highest-friction field on a B2B enquiry form
and would cost conversions on cold traffic, which is exactly what the ~5%
forecast depends on. It carries a "Not sure yet" option for buyers still
scoping. Passing it into the dataLayer means leads can be scored and bid
against by value without any extra tagging work.

Point the Google Ads conversion action at that `generate_lead` event.

Every CTA also carries a `data-cta` attribute so click tracking can be attached
without touching the markup (`hero-demo`, `midpage-demo`, `sticky-demo`, …).
All six pages use the same names, because they run the same CTA scheme:
**Book a Demo** as the primary action everywhere, with **Request Pricing** as
the secondary pill in the hero only. C4–C6 relabel the hero and mid-page
primary as **Book a Side-by-Side Demo** / **Book a Comparison Demo** to match
the competitor ad copy, but the `data-cta` names and the target are unchanged.

One thing to watch when C2 goes live: the C2 ad copy is pricing-led
("See Pricing & Specifications", "Request LHR machine pricing", and a
**Request Pricing** sitelink), while the page now leads with Book a Demo.
That is a deliberate choice, but it is a message-match gap — the click promises
a price and the page asks for a visit. Either point the pricing-led ads and
sitelink at `#demo` knowing the label differs, or soften those ad assets toward
the demo. Worth watching Quality Score and bounce on that ad group in the first
fortnight.

### 3. Claims to sign off

**C1.** Three figures come from Sciton's own JOULE page and are attributed on
the page ("Figures published by Sciton"):

* JOULE owners average **3×** their monthly lease payment
* **11 modules** to choose from
* **95%** of Sciton lasers are still in use today

**C2.** Three figures come from Sciton's own OMNI page, attributed the same way:

* **5,000 W** output
* Wavelengths **760 nm**, an **810/940 nm** blend and **1060 nm**
* **No consumable cost** per treatment (Spritz replaces the gel step)

Confirm all six are approved for UK paid search before the campaign runs.

Two wordings on C2 were deliberately softened and should stay that way unless
Sciton signs off the stronger version:

* Sciton's own copy says OMNI "uses the largest spot sizes on the market".
  That is a comparative superiority claim against unnamed competitors, which
  Google Ads will not reliably accept. The page says **"an extra-large spot
  size"** instead.
* Sciton's US copy cites **110 V** compatibility. That is a US figure and must
  not appear on a UK page. The page says OMNI installs into a standard
  treatment room "without a special electrical supply", and defers the number
  to the specialist. **Get the UK supply spec and add it** — equipment buyers
  ask.

The two handpieces are labelled by function (**large-area** / **precision**)
rather than by spot dimension. The source images are named `31x31` and `10x10`,
which is strong evidence but not sign-off. Add the millimetre figures to the
cards and the comparison table once confirmed.

**C3.** Every figure on the page comes from Sciton's own HALO and
ProFractional pages and is attributed as "Specifications as published by
Sciton":

* HALO **2940 nm** ablative (tunable 20–100 µm) and **1470 nm** non-ablative
  (250–700 µm), delivered in one pass; beam **15 / 7 / 4 mm**; skin types
  **I–VI**; **1–2 treatments**; normal activity **within 24 hours**
* ProFractional Er:YAG ablation **25–1,500 µm**, spot **25 µm / 430 µm**,
  coagulation set independently; **1–3 treatments** plus annual maintenance

Two claims Sciton publishes were deliberately **left off** C3 and should only
go on with sign-off:

* **"59% higher revenue per treatment than other lasers in its category"** — a
  comparative revenue claim against unnamed competitors, the same policy
  problem as OMNI's "largest spot sizes on the market". The commercial case is
  argued qualitatively instead.
* **"the world's only Hybrid Fractional Laser"** — a superiority claim Google
  Ads will not reliably accept. The page says "ablative and non-ablative,
  delivered in the same pass", which is the same fact without the superlative.

The ~80% "Worth It" patient rating is also omitted: it is patient-sentiment
data with no source cited on the UK page.

`#modules` carries **two cards only** — HALO and ProFractional — matching the
ad group. Sciton's wider resurfacing range (HALO TRIBRID, MOXI, ClearSilk) is
deliberately not shown: TRIBRID runs on JOULE X rather than the JOULE chassis
the other two share, and MOXI and ClearSilk pull the page away from the
fractional and CO2 search intent it is bought against.

The `#erbium` section states that Sciton does not build a CO2 laser and
explains why, using Sciton's own absorption chart (`pf-absorption.png`, taken
from the ProFractional page). No competitor is named and no competitor
specification is asserted — but confirm the wording is approved, because it is
the page's answer to the **"CO2 Laser Machines UK"** ad headline and will be
read as a comparison.

### 4. Comparison tables

Both `#compare` tables use **Sciton's own published specifications only** — no
competitor claims, which keeps them clear of Google Ads comparative-advertising
policy.

* **C1** compares JOULE against mJOULE. Worth a sanity check against current UK
  spec sheets, particularly the mJOULE power requirement (listed as 110 V on
  sciton.uk, which looks like a US figure).
* **C2** compares OMNI against BBL HEROic — Sciton's two hair-removal routes.
  The BBL rows are deliberately qualitative because per-pulse figures for BBL
  are not published alongside OMNI's; peak power reads "Not applicable — light
  source, not a laser" rather than inventing a comparable number. If Sciton can
  supply matched figures, the table will take them.
* **C3** compares HALO against ProFractional — Sciton's two resurfacing
  routes — and frames it as a sequencing decision (most clinics buy one and add
  the other) rather than an either/or.

* **C4, C5 and C6** use a different table shape — `.lp-compare--vs`. Column two
  is Sciton's published specification; column three is a **question**, not a
  claim. See section 7.

The strategy doc calls for comparison charts on the competitor ad groups. That
is what `.lp-compare--vs` delivers, without asserting a rival's specifications
on a Sciton page.

### 5. Imagery

* **C1** `#results` uses Sciton's published patient before/after images, with
  physician attribution on the first pair. Confirm they are cleared for UK use
  and that the remaining two pairs have the attribution they need.
* **C2** has **no before/after section**. Sciton publishes no OMNI before/after
  pairs, and the C1 images are BBL HEROic results — putting them on an OMNI page
  would misattribute them. `#areas` carries in-treatment photography instead. If
  cleared OMNI before/after imagery becomes available, that section is where it
  should go, and it is the single biggest thing missing from the page.
* **C3** `#results` carries four attributed before/after pairs pulled from
  sciton.uk — two HALO (Andrew Campbell, MD; Diane Duncan, MD, FACS) and two
  ProFractional (Todd Bessinger, MD, PhD; R. James Koch, MD). Confirm all four
  are cleared for UK use. Note that **only one published HALO pair is a face** —
  the Campbell pair is body skin, and is labelled "Body" on the card rather
  than implied to be facial. If cleared facial HALO pairs exist, they are the
  better swap.
* **C3** omits **Contour TRL**, which the campaign plan lists in the structured
  snippet. There is no Contour TRL page or asset on sciton.uk, so the page does
  not claim it. Either drop it from the snippet or supply UK material for it.

### 6. Publishing

* All six pages ship with `<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`
  and a canonical pointing at the organic page — `https://sciton.uk/joule/` for
  C1, `https://sciton.uk/omni-laser-hair-removal-by-sciton/` for C2,
  `https://sciton.uk/halo-laser-by-sciton/` for C3, and `https://sciton.uk/joule/`
  again for C4, C5 and C6 (the landing-page URL the campaign plan gives for all
  three competitor ad groups). Keep them — a paid landing page should not
  compete with the organic page. The `noindex` matters more on C4–C6 than
  anywhere else: a competitor-named page that gets indexed is a brand problem,
  not just an SEO one.
* Decide the final paths (e.g. `/lp/joule-platform/`, `/lp/omni-hair-removal/`,
  `/lp/fractional-resurfacing/`, `/lp/compare-cynosure/`, `/lp/compare-fotona/`,
  `/lp/compare-platforms/`).
* The C3 campaign plan gives `sciton.uk/halo` as the landing page URL and lists
  **ProFractional System** and **HALO Laser System** as separate sitelinks. This
  page covers both, so either point both sitelinks at it with different anchors
  (`#halo`, `#profractional`) or keep them on the organic product pages.
* The campaign plan lists `sciton.uk/roi` and `sciton.uk/demo` as sitelinks. If
  those pages don't exist, the references here route to the on-page form
  instead.
* C2's budget bands (£20k–£100k+) are set from the strategy doc's £20k–£160k
  equipment range, not from an OMNI price list. Adjust once real pricing is
  confirmed — the bands are what leads get scored on.

### 7. Competitor pages — what may and may not be claimed

C4, C5 and C6 are conquesting pages. They name a rival, which changes what the
page is allowed to do — under Google Ads comparative-advertising policy and
under ordinary trade-mark practice. The rules below are not stylistic. Breaking
one of them is what gets an ad group disapproved or a letter sent.

**No competitor branding, at all.** No logo, no wordmark, no device photograph,
no brand colour, no typeface borrowed from their site, no screenshot of their
material. The names appear in body type, styled exactly like the sentence
around them, and nowhere else. Every occurrence on all three pages is inside a
`<p>`, `<h1>`, `<button>` or `<meta>` — grep before you ship:

```bash
grep -o -E '.{0,60}(Cynosure|Fotona|Lumenis|Cutera|Candela).{0,20}' c[456]-*/index.html
```

**No competitor specifications are asserted.** Sciton does not publish another
manufacturer's numbers, so these pages do not state them. The comparison table
(`.lp-compare--vs`) puts Sciton's published specification in column two and a
**question for the buyer to put to the other supplier** in column three —
"What is the disposable cost per treatment?" rather than a figure. That is
honest, it is useful to a buyer, and it is outside the reach of comparative-
advertising policy because no comparison of fact is being made.

*To upgrade a cell later:* replace the question with that manufacturer's own
published statement, add a numbered footnote under the table giving the source
and the date it was accessed, and get it signed off before it runs. Each table
carries a comment marking the spot.

**Every page carries a non-affiliation line and a trade-mark notice.** The
non-affiliation line sits directly under the H1 (`.lp-disclaimer`); the trade-
mark notice sits above the footer's legal row. Both are required. If you edit
the hero, do not lose the first one.

**The claims on these pages are C1's and C3's claims, already signed off.**
No new figure was introduced. The Sciton column reuses: 11 modules, 3× monthly
lease payment, 95% still in use (C1); HALO 2940/1470 nm, 20–100 µm and
250–700 µm, 15/7/4 mm, I–VI, 24 hours, ProFractional 25–1,500 µm at 25/430 µm
with independent coagulation (C3); OMNI's no-consumable-cost claim (C2). The
one wording to watch is **"11 modules"** next to a treatment list — the list
names six applications, the figure counts modules. Keep both, do not merge them
into "eleven treatments".

**What was deliberately left off.** Nothing about a competitor's price, market
share, install base, reliability or clinical outcomes. No "better than", no
"more than", no superlatives pointed at a named rival. The C4 ad copy includes
"Better Results Than Cynosure" and "JOULE handles more treatments than Cynosure
Elite" — the **page** does not make those claims, and the gap is deliberate. If
the ad assets stay as written, expect scrutiny on that ad group; the safer move
is to soften the two headlines toward the page's own framing.

**Model names.** The pages name manufacturers, never models — no Elite, no 4D,
no M22. Adding a model name narrows the claim to a specific product and raises
the sign-off burden, without improving the match to a search for the brand.

## Design notes

Everything is measured from the live site rather than inferred.

| Token | Value |
|---|---|
| Dark / slab | `#252525` |
| Light band | `#f7f7f7` |
| Body text | `#717171` |
| De-emphasised | `#a9a9a9` |
| Hairline | `#e5e5e5` |
| Brand blue | `#003595` — hover/active only, never a resting fill |

Type is Aeonik throughout. Hierarchy is built from scale, not weight: every
heading from `hdg-giant` down to `hdg-4` is weight 500. Cards are 8px radius
with `3px 14px 64px rgba(0,0,0,.12)`; buttons are full pills.

Motion is split the way the site splits it: `0.15s ease-in-out` for anything
the cursor touches, `0.5s ease` for the FAQ disclosure, and the theme's own
`.js-fade` reveal for content. Reveals are driven by `IntersectionObserver`
and everything animates on `transform`/`opacity` only.

### Two deliberate departures from sciton.uk

1. **No scroll-jacking.** The site's `treatment-cards` component is a
   GSAP-pinned horizontal scroller holding the viewport for its duration. The
   card design is reproduced exactly, but laid out as a responsive grid. Pinned
   scrollers cost conversions and behave poorly on mobile.
2. **No navigation links.** The header keeps the Sciton shell but drops the
   nav, country switcher and hamburger. The footer is stripped the same way:
   no social icons, no link columns, no Google Maps link. Address and phone
   stay as contact details, and the Privacy Policy is the single outbound
   link (opens in a new tab). On a paid landing page every other outbound
   link is a lost click.

The in-page anchor nav is hidden below 1024px, where it has no links to show;
a sticky bottom CTA bar takes over on mobile.

### Hero ambient orbs

The hero uses the site's `.blob` layer — three `#44bdff` orbs at 15% opacity
with a 100px glow, drifting on a 35s loop. On sciton.uk that layer is
`position: fixed` at `z-index: -1`, so it floats behind the whole page. Here
it is scoped to the hero (section 16 of `lp.css`). To make it page-wide
instead, delete that block and move the `.blob` div to be the first child of
`<body>`. It respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Sciton iQ reels (C1 only)

The dashboard reels counter-slide as the section passes through the viewport
— top reel right, bottom reel left, ±15% — matching the "Intelligence That
Works For You" section on sciton.com/joule. Driven by a passive `scroll`
listener in `initReels()` (`assets/js/lp.js`) doing one `getBoundingClientRect`
read and two `transform` writes per event; no layout is invalidated and no
`requestAnimationFrame` loop runs. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the reels
stay at the theme's static -15% / +15% rest state.

C2 has no reels section — Sciton does not publish an iQ integration for OMNI,
so the claim isn't made. `initReels()` finds no `.graphic-screens` and returns
without binding a listener.

### CTA arrows

`.btn-action` normally draws an arrow from two pseudo-elements — `:before` is
the 42px dark disc, `:after` the white chevron. Both are suppressed in
`lp.css` (section 17) and the 4rem right padding that reserved the arrow lane
is rebalanced so labels centre in the pill. Removing the arrow from the markup
alone would not have worked; it is entirely CSS-generated.

### Contrast

The site uses `#a9a9a9` for the de-emphasised half of its two-tone headings.
That reads well on the `#252525` bands (6.5:1) but only reaches 2.35:1 on
white, below WCAG AA. On light sections those greys are stepped up to
`#717171` (4.9:1), which is what sciton.uk itself uses on light grounds.

The same problem runs the other way, and it was missed until the listing page
was built. The theme's `.wysiwyg` paragraph colour is `#717171`, correct on
white but only 2.9:1 inside a `#252525` band. It went unnoticed because the
statistics band's stat list sets `text-gray-200` explicitly — only the prose
column beside it was affected. Section 24 of `lp.css` steps bare paragraphs
and list items up to `#a9a9a9` on the dark bands, which puts them at 6.5:1.
Anything carrying its own colour class is left alone, and the white module
cards that sit inside those bands are untouched. **This changed C1, C2 and C3
as well as the new pages** — it is a fix, not a restyle, but it is a visible
one.

### What C2 adds to `lp.css`

C2 introduces no new colours, radii or shadows, and reuses C1's components
wherever the shape fits. It needed exactly three additions, all annotated in
`lp.css`:

* **`.lp-stat-num`** — C1's statistics band uses outlined-numeral SVGs
  (`stat-3x.svg` and friends) taken from the live site. There are no equivalent
  assets for `5000 W`, `3λ` or `£0`, so C2 renders those figures as text in the
  same 8rem lane the images occupy on C1.
* **`.page-omni-lp .lp-module__image`** — the module card is reused for OMNI's
  handpieces, but Sciton supplies those three renders at three different aspect
  ratios. The image box is squared off with `object-fit: contain` so the card
  headings stay on one baseline. Scoped to the C2 body class so C1's cards are
  untouched.
* **`.lp-module` on a three-card row** — no change needed; the grid already
  reflows from six cards to three.

`#areas` adds no CSS at all. It reuses `.lp-ba__frame` and `.lp-ba__label` from
C1's before/after component — the 3:4 crop and the white corner pill are exactly
what an area tile wants, so the area name goes in the label slot.

The one shared-file change C2 required was in `lp.js`: the `generate_lead`
`campaign` value now reads from `data-campaign` on the form instead of being
hard-coded, so both pages run off one script. C1 carries
`data-campaign="C1-sciton-brand"` and pushes exactly what it did before.

### What C3 adds to `lp.css`

Two rules, both scoped to the `.page-halo-lp` body class so C1 and C2 are
untouched:

* **`.page-halo-lp .hero-banner-new`** (section 19) — the theme's hero carries
  no vertical padding. On C1 and C2 the tall portrait product render sets the
  band height and the shorter copy column sits inside it with room to spare;
  C3's HALO render is landscape, so the copy column is the taller of the two
  and ran flush to the top and bottom edges of the band. The C3 hero gets
  4rem/5rem of its own at `lg`, 3rem at the foot on small screens.
* **`.page-halo-lp .lp-modules`** (section 19) — the shared modules grid goes
  to three columns at `lg`, which left C3's two cards stranded against an empty
  third column. C3 takes two columns, capped at 820px and centred, so the cards
  keep the width they have on C1 and C2.

Everything else on C3 is an existing component: `.lp-trustbar`, `.lp-modules`,
`.lp-stat-num` (borrowed from C2 for `2λ`, `1500 µm` and `24h`), `.lp-compare`,
`.lp-quotes`, `.lp-ba` and the theme's `alternating-content`,
`treatment-cards`, `statistics`, `top-heavy-cta` and `content-accordion`
blocks. `lp.js` needed no change at all — `data-campaign` already drives the
`generate_lead` push.

C3 uses `#44bdff` for the hero orbs, which is the colour sciton.uk sets on the

### What C4, C5 and C6 add to `lp.css`

Three annotated blocks, sections 20–22, all scoped to the new body classes
(`.page-vs-cynosure`, `.page-vs-fotona`, `.page-vs-platforms`) so C1–C3 are
untouched. No new colour, radius, shadow or timing.

* **`.lp-criteria`** (section 20) — the "six questions" grid. A text-only card
  on the same surface as `.lp-module`: white, 24px radius, the theme's
  `3px 14px 64px rgba(0,0,0,.12)`. One, two and three columns at the theme's

* **`.lp-compare--vs`** (section 21) — the comparison-table modifier. Column

* **Hero spacing and grids** (section 22) — C4–C6 get the vertical padding C3

### Why the competitor pages are not clones of each other

Three near-identical pages would be worse for the buyer and worse for Quality
Score. Each keeps the spine but changes what it argues:

* **C4** leads on breadth — treatments per chassis, what the next indication
  costs, six module cards. Criteria and table rows lean commercial.
* **C5** leads on resurfacing depth — it is the only one of the three with the
  hybrid-wavelength and water-absorption sections (`halo-wavelengths.png`,
  `pf-absorption.png`, both reused from C3), a four-card module row, and
  criteria and table rows written in microns rather than in modules.
* **C6** leads on consolidation — broadband light and laser on one chassis, and
  a switching section written for a clinic that already owns two or three
  systems rather than one.

All three names on C6 appear once, in the H1, then the argument runs
generically about single- and dual-wavelength systems. That was a deliberate
choice over three per-brand blocks: one ad group, one landing page, and no
section thin enough to read as filler.

### The listing page

`index.html` is not a landing page and is not part of the campaign. It is the
front door we present from, and what a Vercel deployment serves at `/`.

Six cards, one per campaign, each carrying a real screenshot of the page, the
ad-group code, the angle in a sentence, three facts and a link. That is the
whole page — masthead, cards, footer. It deliberately does not restate the
open items from sections 1, 3 and 7 above: those are for the room, not for the
screen, and a page that argues its own case reads defensively.

Its CSS is section 23 of `lp.css`, scoped to `.page-index` so nothing on it can
reach a live page. `.lp-card` reuses `.lp-module`'s surface — white, 24px
radius, the theme's `3px 14px 64px` shadow — with the whole card made clickable
by a pseudo-element on the heading link, so the accessible name stays the
heading rather than becoming the card's entire text. One thing to know if you
edit it: the hover lift is armed by `.lp-card.js-animated`, not by `.lp-card`.
The theme animates the reveal transform over 1s, and because `lp.css` loads
after the theme, declaring a 0.15s transform transition up front would cut the
reveal short.

The previews are generated, not drawn. Rerun `.claude/shots.py` after changing
a hero, or the card will quietly show the old page.
