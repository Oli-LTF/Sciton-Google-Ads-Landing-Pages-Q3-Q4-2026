The real question is not which box to buy. It is how many boxes you end up with.

Light and laser, one chassis

Broadband light for the volume work. Lasers for everything it cannot reach.

Clinics comparing intense-light systems are usually solving for throughput: pigmentation, vascular concerns, acne and hair reduction across large areas, delivered by a team member rather than the practice owner. BBL® HEROic™ is Sciton's answer to that — automated pulse placement, full-body areas in minutes, delegatable to staff of any experience level.

What changes the commercial picture is that the same chassis also takes lasers. Er:YAG resurfacing at 2940 nm, non-ablative work at 1470 nm and 1064 nm, 1927 nm for prejuvenation, 532 nm for vessels. Broadband light and laser are configured on one platform, in one room, on one service contract — rather than bought as separate systems eighteen months apart.

That is the whole argument. Not that any single wavelength is better, but that a clinic buying one wavelength at a time pays for the chassis, the training and the floor space every time.

What to compare

Six questions to put to every manufacturer on your list.

Including us. Ask all of them the same six, in writing, before anyone demonstrates anything.

  • 01

    How many wavelengths are on the chassis?

    Count what the system delivers today, in nanometres. A platform sold on one or two wavelengths is a platform you will be adding to.

  • 02

    Do light and laser run from the same system?

    Broadband light covers the volume work; lasers cover what it cannot reach. If they need two systems, you are buying two of everything around them.

  • 03

    What does the next indication cost?

    Ask whether a new modality is a module on the platform you already own, or a second capital purchase with its own footprint, training and service contract.

  • 04

    What is the consumable cost per treatment?

    Tips, gels, cartridges and filters are the line that quietly decides your margin. Ask for the twelve-month figure at your expected volume, not the unit price.

  • 05

    Who installs, trains and services it here?

    Ask where the engineer is based, what the response time is, where parts ship from, and what a week of downtime does to your treatment diary.

  • 06

    What is it worth in five years?

    Ask how many systems of that generation are still in clinical use, and whether the platform can be upgraded rather than replaced when the range moves on.

The commercial case

One platform that keeps earning beats four that each earn once.

Purchase price is the easiest number to compare and the least useful one. What decides whether a system pays is how much of the demand walking through your door it can serve, and what the second and third indication cost to add.

A single-application device earns from one line on the price list. A modular platform earns from several, and each module you add reuses training, floor space and a service contract you have already paid for.

Switching platform

Already own two or three systems? That is the conversation to have.

Consolidating onto one platform is usually a better discussion than replacing like for like. It frees a treatment room, removes a service contract or two, and puts your team on one interface instead of three.

Your UK specialist will work through the whole of it before you commit: what your existing systems are worth and whether they can be part-exchanged, how the install fits around your treatment diary, how long your team needs on training before they are treating unsupervised, what the service contract covers, and which finance or lease structure suits the way the clinic is funded.

Who may operate a Class IV laser in your clinic, and under what supervision, is set by your registration and your local authority licensing. That gets worked through alongside the training plan rather than after it.
